Ever since Olympic athletes started showing up with those distinctive circular marks, cupping has gone from ancient practice to mainstream recovery tool. If you're curious but hesitant, here's exactly what to expect from a [fire cupping session](/services/fire-cupping/) at our clinic.

## What cupping is

Cupping is a Traditional Chinese Medicine technique in which glass cups are briefly warmed with a flame and placed on the skin, creating gentle suction as the air inside cools. Where massage presses *into* tissue, cupping *lifts* it — decompressing muscle and fascia, drawing blood flow into the area, and releasing tension that pressure alone can struggle to reach.

At our Windermere clinic, cupping is combined with **Gua Sha** (a smooth-edged tool gliding over the muscles) and massage therapy in a single session, so you get the benefit of all three approaches.

## What it feels like

Most first-timers are surprised: the sensation is a firm, warm *pulling* — most people find it strangely pleasant, like a stretch from the inside out. Cups may be left in place for several minutes or glided across oiled skin like a reverse massage.

The flame never touches you; it only warms the air inside the cup for a moment before placement.

## About those marks

The circular marks are the most famous part of cupping — and the most misunderstood. They are **not bruises** from impact. The suction draws blood toward the surface, and where circulation is stagnant or tension is dense, the marks tend to be darker.

- They are typically **painless**
- They fade in **3–7 days** depending on your circulation
- Practitioners read them as a map of where your tension lives

If you have a wedding, photo shoot or beach vacation coming up, schedule your cupping session accordingly.

## Who benefits most

Cupping is especially popular with:

- **Athletes** managing training load and recovery
- People with **dense, stubborn upper back and shoulder tension** that massage alone hasn't fully released
- Desk workers with that immovable **"knot between the shoulder blades"**
- Clients who plateau with regular massage and want a different stimulus

## Good to know before you book

Stay hydrated before and after your session, avoid intense workouts the same day, and keep the area warm and covered afterwards. Cupping isn't recommended over broken skin or for people with certain blood conditions — mention any health concerns when you book, and our practitioners will advise you.
