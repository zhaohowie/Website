Standing at the booking page wondering which massage to pick? You're not alone — "deep tissue or relaxation?" is one of the questions we answer most. Here's a practical way to decide.

## Start with your goal, not the menu

Ask yourself one question: **do I want to fix something, or do I want to unwind?**

- *"My shoulders have been in knots for months"* → treatment-focused. Book [deep tissue](/services/deep-tissue-massage/) or [therapeutic massage](/services/therapeutic-massage/).
- *"I'm exhausted and wound up; I need to reset"* → relaxation-focused. Book a [Swedish massage](/services/swedish-massage/) or [hot stone massage](/services/hot-stone-massage/).

Both are performed by registered massage therapists and both qualify for direct billing — the difference is technique and intention, not legitimacy.

## What deep tissue actually feels like

Deep tissue uses slow strokes and sustained, firm pressure to reach deeper muscle layers. On chronically tight areas it can feel intense — a "good hurt" — but it should never have you gritting your teeth. Your RMT will check in about pressure, and you should speak up freely; effective treatment doesn't require suffering.

Expect possible mild soreness the next day, similar to a workout. Drink water, move gently, and the looseness that follows is worth it.

## What relaxation massage actually does

Don't let the word "relaxation" make it sound trivial. Swedish massage measurably calms the nervous system — slower breathing, lower tension, better sleep that night. If your tension is driven by stress rather than physical strain, a relaxation massage often does more for you than force ever could.

[Hot stone massage](/services/hot-stone-massage/) is the deluxe version: heat penetrates muscles and releases tension that would otherwise need much firmer pressure — a favourite through Edmonton winters.

## You don't actually have to choose

Here's the secret: a skilled RMT blends techniques all the time. Book a [therapeutic massage](/services/therapeutic-massage/), describe what's going on, and your therapist will use firm work where your body needs it and flowing, relaxing work everywhere else. Many regulars settle into this as their default.

## A simple rule of thumb

- **First massage ever?** Start Swedish. You can always go deeper next time.
- **Specific, stubborn problem area?** Deep tissue.
- **Bit of both, or not sure?** Therapeutic — and let your RMT tailor it.
