Back pain is the single most common reason people walk through our doors in Windermere. And one of the most common questions we hear at the front desk is: *"Should I book a massage or try acupuncture?"*

The honest answer is that both can help — but they work differently, and the right choice depends on what's behind your pain.

## When massage therapy is the better starting point

Massage therapy works directly on muscles and soft tissue. It tends to be the better first choice when:

- Your pain feels **muscular** — tightness, stiffness, or knots you can point to
- The pain started after **physical activity**, lifting, or a long day of yard work
- You sit at a desk all day and feel it in your **lower back and hips**
- You want to feel the difference **immediately** — most people leave a [therapeutic massage](/services/therapeutic-massage/) feeling looser the same day

For long-standing, stubborn tension, [deep tissue massage](/services/deep-tissue-massage/) reaches the deeper muscle layers that everyday tightness builds up in.

## When acupuncture is worth trying

[Acupuncture](/services/acupuncture/) approaches pain through the nervous system and the body's own regulatory responses. Our registered acupuncturists often see good results when:

- Pain is **chronic** and hasn't fully responded to other treatments
- The pain **radiates** or is hard to localize — you can't quite put a finger on it
- Stress, poor sleep or tension headaches come **along with** the back pain
- You prefer a **drug-free** approach to pain management

Acupuncture is quite gentle — most clients feel little more than a brief pinch, and many find sessions deeply relaxing.

## The case for combining both

In practice, many of our long-term clients with recurring back pain alternate between the two: massage to release the muscular component, acupuncture to calm the pain response and address contributing factors like stress and sleep. Because our clinic offers both under one roof, your therapists can coordinate your care.

If your back pain involves numbness, weakness in the legs, or follows a fall or accident, see a physician first — and if it's from a car accident, ask us about [MVA treatment](/services/mva-treatment/), which is billed through your motor vehicle accident claim.

Book a [therapeutic massage](/services/therapeutic-massage/) as a starting point and tell your RMT what's going on. They'll assess your back and tell you honestly whether massage, acupuncture, or a combination will serve you best. Both treatments are eligible for direct billing to most insurance plans.
