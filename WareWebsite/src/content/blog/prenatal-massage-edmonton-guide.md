Pregnancy asks a lot of your body. As your centre of gravity shifts week by week, the lower back, hips, and legs take on loads they've never carried — and by the third trimester, "just a little sore" can become "I can't get comfortable anywhere." [Maternity massage](/services/maternity-massage/) exists precisely for this.

## Is prenatal massage safe?

For most healthy pregnancies, yes — prenatal massage by an RMT experienced in maternity care is considered safe and is one of the most-recommended comfort measures for pregnancy aches. Two important caveats:

- If your pregnancy is **high-risk** or has complications, check with your doctor or midwife before booking.
- Always **tell your therapist you're pregnant** (even early on) and how far along you are, so positioning and techniques are adapted properly.

Our RMTs are experienced in both prenatal and postnatal massage and adjust every session to how you're feeling that day.

## How you'll actually lie on the table

The most common question: *"How does a massage even work with a bump?"* The answer is **side-lying positioning** — you rest comfortably on your side, supported by a system of cushions under your head, belly and knees. It's the same position most people naturally sleep in during late pregnancy, and many clients say the supported side-lying setup alone is the most comfortable they've been in weeks.

## What prenatal massage helps most

- **Lower back and hip pain** from shifting posture and loosening ligaments
- **Leg cramps, heaviness and mild swelling**
- **Neck and shoulder tension** (often worse with pregnancy insomnia)
- **Sleep quality** — the calming effect on the nervous system is real, and rest is the foundation of a healthy pregnancy
- **Stress** — an hour where the only job is to be comfortable

## When to start and how often

Most clients begin regular prenatal massage in the **second trimester** and continue through the third, often increasing frequency as the due date approaches — monthly at first, then biweekly, then weekly in the final stretch for many. And don't forget **postnatal massage**: carrying, feeding and rocking a newborn creates its own neck and shoulder tension that deserves care too.
