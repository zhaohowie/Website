"How often should I come back?" It's the question every client asks at the end of a great massage — and the honest answer is: *it depends on what you're treating*. Here's the framework our RMTs use when they build treatment plans.

## Recovering from an injury or acute pain: weekly to start

When you're dealing with an active problem — a strained back, a seized neck, post-accident recovery — infrequent massage is like watering a plant once a season. Muscles pull back toward their guarded pattern between sessions, so early on, **weekly or biweekly** visits keep progress compounding instead of resetting.

As pain decreases and movement returns, your therapist will space visits out. A typical arc looks like: weekly → biweekly → monthly → as needed.

## Chronic tension: every 2–3 weeks

For long-standing issues — the desk-job shoulders, the perpetually tight lower back — **every two to three weeks** tends to be the sweet spot. It's frequent enough to keep tension from fully rebuilding, while your body gradually learns a looser baseline.

This is also where [deep tissue massage](/services/deep-tissue-massage/) earns its keep, progressively working through layers that a single session can't fully address.

## Stress management and general wellness: monthly

If massage is your pressure-release valve rather than a treatment for something specific, **once a month** is the classic rhythm — and it lines up nicely with how most benefit plans are structured. A monthly [Swedish](/services/swedish-massage/) or [hot stone massage](/services/hot-stone-massage/) is genuine preventive self-care: better sleep, lower baseline tension, and a standing appointment that forces you to pause.

## Make your benefits do the work

Most extended health plans in Alberta include an annual massage maximum — commonly **$300 to $1,000**. Divided across a year, that often funds a visit every four to six weeks with little or no out-of-pocket cost, especially with our [direct billing](/faq/). And since acupuncture usually has its own separate maximum, alternating [massage and acupuncture](/services/acupuncture/) can effectively double your covered visits.

One caution: don't leave your coverage until December. Every year, clients discover unused benefits with three weeks left to use them — book earlier and spread the benefit through the year.

## The real answer

The best frequency is one you'll actually keep. A realistic monthly appointment beats an ambitious weekly plan you abandon by February. Tell your RMT your goals and your budget — they'll design a plan around both.
